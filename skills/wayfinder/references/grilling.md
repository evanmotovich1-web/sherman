# Grilling

How a grilling ticket is worked. Loaded by `wayfinder`; useful anywhere a
decision has to come out of a person rather than out of a document.

## The rule that makes it work

**One question at a time.** Not a numbered list of six, not a question with
three sub-questions bolted on. One, then wait, then let the answer choose the
next one.

A list of questions gets a list of answers, each shallower than it would have
been alone, and it forecloses the follow-up that was actually going to find the
thing. A single question gets thought about.

## Never answer your own question

This is the load-bearing rule, and it is the one under pressure, because
answering yourself is faster and usually plausible.

A grilling ticket resolves **only** through live exchange with the person who
speaks for the decision. Filling in their side — even with a well-reasoned
guess, even marked as a guess — produces a map that records agreement which
never happened, and every ticket built on it inherits that.

If the person is not available, the ticket stays open. That is the correct
outcome, not a failure to work around.

## What to ask

Grill toward the decision, not around it.

- **Ask for the specific over the general.** "What happens when a sample arrives
  without a requisition?" beats "what are the edge cases?"
- **Ask what would have to be true.** When someone states a preference, the
  useful next question is what would change their mind.
- **Follow the surprise.** When an answer does not fit your model, that gap is
  the most valuable thing in the conversation. Chase it before moving on.
- **Ask about the thing that already went wrong.** Real constraints come from
  history, and people will describe them concretely when abstract questions get
  abstract answers.
- **Name a disagreement when you find one.** Two people describing the same
  process differently is a finding, not noise to smooth over.

## When to stop

Stop when the question the ticket names has an answer that would survive being
written down and read by someone else in six months. Not when the conversation
runs out of energy, and not when you have enough to start building — the ticket
resolves a decision, and a decision is either made or it is not.

Write the answer in the person's own vocabulary. If they call it a requisition,
the ticket says requisition.

## The boundary

No PHI in a question, an answer, or a resolution. When a person starts to
describe a specific patient to make a point, take the shape and drop the
specifics: "so the case where the sample and the paperwork disagree" — and say
plainly that the details cannot be recorded here.
